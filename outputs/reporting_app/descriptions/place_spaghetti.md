## Place trend plot

This visual focuses on a single Place and shows how reported rates have changed over time for the selected metric.

The chart is designed to help Places understand their own trajectory whilst providing optional national and peer context. As with all dashboard views, it should be interpreted as an exploratory tool rather than a measure of performance.

Differences between Places may reflect variation in local populations, service models, cohort definitions, implementation approaches, reporting practices and data quality. The chart is therefore intended to support learning and hypothesis generation rather than formal performance assessment.

## What the chart shows

The selected Place is displayed as a highlighted blue line, showing the monthly rate per 1,000 patients for the chosen metric.

Hovering over a data point displays additional information including:

- Place name
- Reporting month
- Reported rate per 1,000 patients
- Engagement count
- Caseload

The selected Place is always shown, regardless of any additional overlays or comparison options that are enabled.

### Show matched comparison

An optional **Show matched comparison** overlay displays rates for statistically matched comparison population.

The comparison population is constructed from high-frailty patients linked to PCNs outside participating NNHIP Places. Comparator PCNs are selected by matching on:

- Percentage of the population aged 65 years or over
- Percentage of the population living in the 20% most deprived areas nationally

Matched PCNs are then combined to create a Place-specific comparison population.

The matched comparison is intended to provide context when interpreting changes in reporting activity over time. It can help you understand whether observed trends appear broadly similar to those seen in a comparable population with similar age and deprivation characteristics.

The comparison is **not a counterfactual** and should not be used to make causal claims about the impact of the NNHIP programme. Differences between the selected Place and the matched comparison may arise from many factors, including local populations, services, implementation approaches and data quality.

Where available, the matched comparison is shown as a dark blue line.

### Show similar trajectories

An optional **Show similar trajectories** overlay displays Places whose reported patterns of change are most similar to the selected Place.

Similarity is assessed by comparing Place trajectories over time after indexing each Place to its own baseline. This allows Places with similar patterns of change to be identified even when their absolute rates differ.

These comparisons are intended to support learning and exploration. Similar trajectories do not necessarily imply that Places have similar populations, services or operational contexts.

Hover information for similar trajectories also includes:

- Trajectory similarity measures
- Number of overlapping reporting months used when calculating similarity

### Show all Places

An optional **Show all Places** overlay displays the trajectories of all participating Places as faint grey lines in the background.

This provides national context and helps illustrate the range of variation reported across Places whilst keeping the selected Place as the primary focus.

The grey trajectories should be interpreted as contextual information rather than direct comparators.

### Cohort engagement period

An optional **Show engagement period** overlay highlights the period following the first month in which the Place reported actively engaging with its cohort.

The shaded area provides a visual distinction between:

- Baseline activity prior to reported engagement
- Activity occurring after cohort engagement began

This can help you explore whether patterns changed following the start of cohort-focused improvement activity.

The marker indicates the first **reported** month of engagement and should not be interpreted as a formal intervention date.

### Population-weighted mean

An optional **population-weighted mean** overlay can be enabled to show the overall national trend.

This is calculated by summing reported numerator values across all Places and dividing by the combined denominator population before converting the result to a rate per 1,000 patients.

Because larger populations contribute proportionally more to the calculation, the resulting value represents national activity rather than an average of Place-level rates.

The population-weighted mean is shown as a highlighted dark blue line.

### Median and inter-quartile range

An optional **median and inter-quartile range (IQR)** overlay can also be enabled.

The median represents the middle Place value for a given month, whilst the IQR shows the range containing the middle 50% of Place values.

These statistics provide context regarding the distribution of reported values across participating places.

These should not be interpreted as targets, thresholds or performance standards.

The median is shown as an amber line and the IQR is shown as a shaded amber ribbon.

## How to interpret trends

The chart is best used to understand how a Place's reported activity evolves over time and how that trajectory relates to wider national patterns.

Particular attention should be paid to:

- The overall direction of travel
- Changes before and after reported cohort engagement
- Whether the Place remains within the range reported by most Places
- Whether Places with similar trajectories are exhibiting comparable patterns
- How the Place compares with national summary measures over time

Observed patterns should be interpreted alongside local knowledge and operational context.

## Why this view is useful

- Places the selected Place at the centre of the analysis
- Provides national context without losing local detail
- Supports exploration of trends before and after cohort engagement
- Enables comparison with Places exhibiting similar trajectories
- Provides optional national benchmark overlays
- Supports learning, hypothesis generation and discussion
- Encourages interpretation of variation without making performance judgements
- Complements national-level views by helping you focus on your local experience
