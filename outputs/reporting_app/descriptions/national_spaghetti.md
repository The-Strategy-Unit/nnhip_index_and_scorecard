## National spaghetti plot

This visual shows how reported rates vary across Places over time. Each line represents a Place and tracks the monthly rate per 1,000 patients for the selected metric.

The purpose of this view is to support exploration of patterns, variation and trends. It is intentionally non-judgemental; differences between Places may reflect a wide range of factors including local populations, service models, recording practices, cohort definitions and data quality. The chart helps you understand variation, not determine whether a Place is performing "well" or "poorly".

### What the chart shows

The chart displays one line for each Place, showing how the selected metric has changed month-by-month.

Place lines are shown using a low-opacity grey colour so that the overall pattern across all Places can be seen without drawing attention to any individual organisation.

Hovering over a line displays additional information including:

- Place name
- Reporting month
- Reported rate per 1,000 patients
- Engagement count
- Caseload

### Population-weighted mean

An optional **population-weighted mean** overlay can be enabled to show the overall national trend.

This is calculated by summing the reported numerator values across all Places and dividing by the combined denominator population before converting the result to a rate per 1,000 patients.

Because the calculation uses the combined population, larger Places contribute proportionally to the national figure. This provides a national summary of activity rather than an average of Place-level rates.

The population-weighted mean is shown as a highlighted blue line.

### Median and inter-quartile range

An optional **median and inter-quartile range (IQR)** overlay can also be enabled.

The median represents the middle Place value for a given month, with half of Places reporting higher values and half reporting lower values.

The shaded IQR band shows the range between the 25th and 75th percentiles, representing the middle 50% of Place values.

These summaries are not targets or benchmarks. They simply help describe how values are distributed across participating Places.

The median is shown as an amber line and the IQR is shown as a shaded amber ribbon.

### High-quality data toggle

When enabled, the chart uses only Places that meet the dashboard's data quality criteria. A Place is considered to have high-quality data if it:

- has submitted data for all reporting months (allowing one missing month), and
- has at least one valid denominator value greater than zero in every submitted month

This allows you to compare metrics derived from all available submissions with metrics derived from the most complete and reliable data. 

When the toggle is disabled, all available Places are included.

## How to interpret variation

Variation between Places should be interpreted carefully.

Differences in reported rates do not necessarily indicate differences in quality, effectiveness or performance. They may arise because Places serve different populations, operate different service models, apply different cohort selection approaches or are at different stages of implementation.

The chart is therefore best used to:

- Explore how rates evolve over time
- Understand the range of values reported nationally
- Identify metrics with relatively consistent or diverse patterns across Places
- Generate questions and hypotheses for further investigation
- Provide context for other benchmarking and analytical views within the dashboard

## Why this view is useful

- Displays all Place trajectories in a single view
- Highlights national patterns while preserving local context
- Supports comparison of current trends with historical reporting periods
- Allows you to explore variation without making value judgements about individual Places
- Provides optional national summary measures to help interpret the overall distribution of reported values
- Helps identify areas that may warrant further investigation alongside other dashboard analyses
